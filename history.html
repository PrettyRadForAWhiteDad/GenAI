Dutch Shepherd Breed Guide

From Dutch Farms to Modern Working Dog

The Dutch Shepherd developed as a practical, multipurpose shepherd and farm dog in the Netherlands. The FCI standard describes dogs that patrolled field borders, moved livestock, guarded farmyards, and even pulled milk carts.

Before 1900

Farm partner

Dogs kept sheep from crops, accompanied flocks, gathered cows, protected kitchen gardens, and alerted farmers to visitors.

12 June 1898

First breed standard

The FCI records this as the date of the breed's first formal standard.

Around 1900

Work changes

As Dutch sheep flocks largely disappeared, the breed's versatility supported a transition into structured training and new service roles.

20th century onward

Expanded roles

Dutch Shepherds were used in police, search, tracking, guide, sport, and continued herding work. Suitability always depends on the individual dog.

21 December 2019

Current FCI standard

The current official standard recognizes short-, long-, and wire-haired varieties under FCI Standard No. 223.

Why versatility endured

The breed's history rewards balanced movement, endurance, alertness, cooperation, and independent problem-solving. Modern owners should translate that heritage into humane outlets rather than assume every dog needs protection work.
